# About Student Space Programs Laboratory (SSPL)

The **Student Space Programs Laboratory (SSPL)** at The Pennsylvania State University provides undergraduate and graduate students the opportunity to design, fabricate, and integrate space flight systems. Founded in **August 2006**, SSPL united a rich history of Penn State student space projects including high-altitude balloons, microgravity payloads, sounding rockets, and NASA Shuttle "Get Away Specials."

---

## 🎯 Our Mission

1. **Provide Hands-On Aerospace Education:** Enable students to apply classroom theoretical knowledge to complex, multi-disciplinary engineering flight projects.
2. **Cultivate Systems Engineering Leadership:** Train students to navigate the complete project lifecycle—from requirements analysis, PDR/CDR reviews, and fabrication to environmental testing and orbital operations.
3. **Advance Space Exploration Research:** Deliver meaningful research contributions to NASA, NSF, DoD, and commercial space entities in lunar resource utilization, CubeSats, and atmospheric science.

---

## 🏛️ Governance & Structure

SSPL operates under a **faculty-directed, student-managed matrix structure**. This model balances academic oversight with complete student ownership of project technical execution.

- **Faculty Direction:** Directed by **Dr. Sven Bilén** (Professor of Engineering Design, Electrical Engineering, and Aerospace Engineering).
- **Student Leadership:** Led by student program managers, subsystem leads, and facility coordinators.
- **Interdisciplinary Reach:** Open to all Penn State majors—Aerospace, Electrical, Mechanical, Computer Science, Materials Science, Industrial Engineering, and Business.

## 📝 Lab History & Milestones

- **2006:** SSPL established at Penn State to consolidate suborbital and satellite projects under a unified systems engineering structure.
- **2012-2018:** Suborbital payload launches aboard NASA sounding rockets at Wallops Flight Facility; OSIRIS & CubeSat design initiatives.
- **2020:** Selected by NASA for Artemis Lunar payload development ($145k award).
- **2023:** Awarded NASA Big Idea Challenge for **SMELT** (Microwave Lunar Ilmenite Smelting).
- **2023-2024:** Participation in NASA/NSF **National Eclipse Ballooning Project (NEBP)**.
- **2026:** Named finalist in NASA's **LunaRecycle Challenge Phase 2**.
